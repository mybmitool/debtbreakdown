Quick Facts

Location: Austin, Texas

Education: BS Finance, University of Texas at Austin

Certification: CFP (Certified Financial Planner)

Experience: 8 years banking + 5 years content

Family: Married, two kids (6 and 9)

Side gig: BBQ smoker collector

Hi, I am James.

I spent eight years at a regional bank in Austin. My job was to open accounts, push credit cards, and meet cross-sell quotas. Every month, my manager posted a leaderboard. Whoever sold the most credit cards got a $100 gift card and a parking spot closer to the door. I won that thing four times.

I thought I was good at my job. Then one afternoon, a woman came in. Late fifties, worked as a cashier. She wanted to consolidate some debt. I pulled up her credit report. Three credit cards — total balance about $11,000. She was paying the minimum on all of them. The interest rates were 23%, 25%, and 27%. She did not know what APR meant.

I explained that if she kept paying the minimum, she would be in debt for about 25 years and pay over $30,000 in interest. She started crying. She said, "I did not know. Nobody told me."

I sat there holding a stack of credit card applications I was supposed to cross-sell. I did not give her one. I helped her apply for a debt consolidation loan at a lower rate. She qualified for 12%. Her payment stayed the same, but she would be out of debt in four years. She thanked me. I felt like a traitor to my bonus.

That night, I went home and told my wife I wanted to quit. She said, "You have been saying that for a year." She was right. I stayed another 18 months. But something had broken.

In 2019, I finally left. No plan. Just a resignation letter and a savings account with about $15,000. My wife was terrified. I was too. But I could not sit at that desk anymore.

I started writing online. My first article was called "Why Your Credit Card Company Hates You." It got 47 views. My mom shared it on Facebook.

Then I wrote about how to calculate credit card interest. That one got a few hundred shares. Someone emailed me: "You explained this better than my bank ever did." That felt good.

I started building tools. Simple calculators. Nothing fancy. Just HTML and JavaScript. A debt payoff calculator. An amortization table. Things I wished I could have handed to people like the woman at the grocery store.

Now I live in Austin with my wife and two kids. I drive a minivan. I grill brisket on weekends. I am not rich. But I do not wake up wondering if I ruined someone’s financial future. That is worth more than a parking spot.
